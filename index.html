Course Description

How can we learn from data, express what remains uncertain, and make useful predictions? This course develops:

  • Bayesian inference, including posterior summaries, decisions, and prediction.
  • Statistical modeling, from simple examples to regression and hierarchical models.
  • Computation and practical implementation using Python.
  • Model criticism, prior sensitivity, and interpretation of uncertainty.
  • Bayesian, frequentist, and Fisherian perspectives on statistical inference.

Objectives

By the end of this course, students should be able to:

  • Formulate a Bayesian model and explain its likelihood, prior, and posterior.
  • Summarize posterior uncertainty and make predictions and decisions.
  • Build and interpret regression and hierarchical models.
  • Use Python for posterior computation and assess the quality of a simulation.
  • Check model assumptions, assess prior sensitivity, and communicate limitations.
  • Distinguish Bayesian, frequentist, and Fisherian interpretations of an analysis.

Course Outline

  • Begin with Why Bayesian?, then follow Agresti’s seven chapters.
  • Approximate unit times below use 60-minute hours. See the Schedule for the proposed teaching-week sequence and hour budgets.
  • Deck 00 is an introductory draft; Decks 01–07 are chapter outlines awaiting development.
00 · Why Bayesian? — 1 hour

Why Bayesian? slides · Orientation

  • Learning from data, expressing uncertainty, and making predictions
  • Bayesian, frequentist, and Fisherian perspectives
  • Assumptions, interpretation, and a cycle of model criticism
01 · Foundations — 4 hours

Foundations slides · Agresti chapter 1

  • Probability models, likelihood, and Bayes’ rule
  • Priors, exchangeability, and conditional independence
  • Posterior summaries, credible intervals, and decisions
  • Prior and posterior prediction; sensitivity and interpretation
02 · Proportions — 4 hours

Proportions slides · Agresti chapter 2

  • Beta–binomial updating and prior choices
  • Posterior intervals, comparisons, and predictive counts
  • Multinomial models and Dirichlet priors
  • Empirical Bayes and an introduction to partial pooling
03 · Means and Normal Models — 5.5 hours

Means and Normal Models slides · Agresti chapter 3

  • Normal models with known and unknown variance
  • Joint, marginal, and conditional posterior distributions
  • Comparing means and introducing multivariate normal structure
  • Exponential families, large-sample behavior, and normal/Laplace approximations
  • Exact calculations and a short software and diagnostics primer
04 · Linear Models — 6 hours

Linear Models slides · Agresti chapter 4

  • Bayesian regression, coefficient priors, and predictive distributions
  • Matrix formulation, categorical predictors, and interactions
  • Prior and posterior predictive checks; cross-validation
  • Regularization, uncertainty, and limits of interpretation
05 · GLMs and Hierarchies — 7 hours

GLMs and Hierarchies slides · Agresti chapter 5

  • Generalized linear models (GLMs), link functions, and response distributions
  • Logistic regression, count models, and overdispersion
  • Exchangeability, partial pooling, and hyperpriors
  • Varying effects, multilevel prediction, and checks at each level
06 · Posterior Computation — 5.5 hours

Posterior Computation slides · Agresti chapter 6

  • Monte Carlo estimates and Monte Carlo error
  • Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), Metropolis–Hastings, and Gibbs sampling
  • Hamiltonian methods and approximation methods
  • Multiple chains, effective sample size, R-hat, and reparameterization
  • Computational diagnostics versus predictive model checks
07 · Model Choice — 4.5 hours

Model Choice slides · Agresti chapter 7

  • Bayes factors, predictive comparison, and model averaging
  • Robustness, sensitivity, and identifiable conclusions
  • Selected mixtures and multivariate models
  • Optional classification, missing-data, network, or Gaussian-process examples
  • An integrative analysis from question to model critique
